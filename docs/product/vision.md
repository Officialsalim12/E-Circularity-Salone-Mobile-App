# Vision

Circular Salone is the system that moves unwanted phones and small electronics in Sierra Leone through collection, assessment, repair, refurbishment, reuse, parts recovery, or recycling.

It is the coordination layer for that work: who has the device, who collects it, what happened to it, and what we can count afterwards. The history of a device is the Circularity Registry.

## The problem

People keep broken, old, or unused phones because they do not know where to take them, whether repair is worth it, or whether the device will actually be recovered. Collection is scattered. Devices that could be repaired get sold or dumped with no assessment. After a device leaves the house, nobody can see what happened to it. The value in parts, repair, and reuse leaves the local economy. Without records we also cannot say how many devices were collected, repaired, reused, or recycled, or who took part.

## What "good" looks like later

Devices stay in use longer. Repair is easier to reach. Reusable phones go back to someone who will use them. Parts and materials are recovered on purpose. Young people can work in the network as Circularity Agents. Households and organizations have a straightforward way to hand a device over. The numbers come from the registry, not from a slide.

## What the first release has to prove

```text
A real device in Sierra Leone
enters the network,
a real agent collects it,
it is registered and assessed,
it goes to a real circular path,
and the journey is on record.
```

If that works in the pilot, we have a base. The first release is there to prove the operation. It is not there to carry extra technology.

## Pilot

Phones, smartphones, feature phones, and small consumer electronics. Freetown first.

These figures are proposals, not a commitment:

- about 1,000 households
- about 10 to 20 agents
- about 5 to 10 repair or refurbishment partners
- recycling partners downstream
- one platform

The exact streets or districts inside Freetown are not fixed. See [open-decisions.md](open-decisions.md).

## What we are actually building

Collection coordination, youth agents, a record per device, repair, refurbishment, reuse, parts recovery, recycling, points, offline field work, and reports that can run on a slow connection. Design it as that network. A pickup form on its own is not the product.

Later we may add device categories, larger businesses, institutional programs, a national network, government reporting, and use outside Sierra Leone. None of that is in the first build unless a later decision adds it. See [roadmap.md](roadmap.md).
